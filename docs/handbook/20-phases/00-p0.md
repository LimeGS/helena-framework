Nothing computes in P0. It freezes a choice, so that a result produced in six
months can still say what it was produced from.

## Before you start

Nothing — this is the first step. But know **which scan** you mean. A scroll
often has several, at different energies and resolutions, and they are not
interchangeable.

## The decisions

1. **Pick the scroll from the catalogue**, on the Scrolls page. The catalogue is
   the frozen list of what this deployment can reach; a scroll not in it is not
   a scroll this platform can start from.
2. **Check the declared scale** — µm per voxel — against the scan you think you
   chose.
3. **Add the scroll to your mission.** Coverage, backlogs and job counts are all
   computed against the mission's scrolls, so a scroll outside it is work that
   will not be counted anywhere.

## Controls

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Refresh catalogue | Re-reads the public bucket rather than the cached inventory. The cache is there because the listing is slow, not because it is authoritative. |
| Add to mission | Scopes the scroll. This is what makes later pages able to count anything. |

## Reading the result

A source snapshot exists, with a URI, a digest and a scale. Everything
downstream binds to it — that binding is what `lineage` means everywhere else in
this handbook.

## Traps

> **Trap** The scale is the expensive one. Every later phase resamples in
> microns, so a detector trained at 7.91 µm fed a stack at 9.362 µm sees the
> wrong physical thickness — and nothing downstream notices. There is no error;
> it simply produces a worse map.

> **Note** A scroll in the catalogue is not a scroll on this host. The catalogue
> says what is reachable, and the first phase that needs bytes is the one that
> discovers whether they arrive.
