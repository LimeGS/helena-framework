---
title: P2 — Geometry certification
summary: Is this surface one sheet of papyrus, or two welded together by a segmenter that lost the thread?
---

A gate, not a measurement. It produces a verdict per surface.

## Before you start

One or more surfaces with no verdict yet. An **imported** surface qualifies too,
and usually needs this more than a grown one: growing certifies at finalisation,
importing skips that entirely, so a catalogue surface arrives unmeasured.

## The decisions

1. **Set the batch size and queue.** It runs over surfaces with no verdict. A
   bounded batch stays reviewable; an unbounded one produces a wall of verdicts
   nobody reads.
2. **Use dry run first on a new scroll.** It lists what would be certified and
   stops, so you see the population before spending the fleet on it.

## Fields

| Field | Required | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `sample` | **yes** | must equal the job's immutable sample; refused at command build, on the worker, after the claim |
| `surface_id` | | certify this one surface only — still only if it carries no verdict |
| `limit` | | how many surfaces in one pass. Defaults to 25 |
| `dry_run` | | list what would be certified and stop |

The panel's own Certification view is read-only; queueing this happens from
elsewhere and is covered in
[Certification, Lineage and Audit](#/docs/panel/certification-and-lineage).

## What the gate actually measures

Not a scorecard, and not a set of independent tests ANDed together — an
**ordered cascade**. The first condition that fires decides the verdict, so a
surface with three problems is named for the first one in this order:

| Order | Fires on | Verdict | Reason recorded |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | interpenetration pairs, or lamina step discontinuities | `GEOMETRY_REJECTED_LAMINA_SWITCH` | `NON_PARALLEL_STAB_OR_STEP_DISCONTINUITY` |
| 2 | near-coincident overlap pairs | `GEOMETRY_REJECTED_BRIDGE` | `NEAR_COINCIDENT_DOUBLED_SURFACE` |
| 3 | a self-intersection | `GEOMETRY_REJECTED_DISTORTION` | `SELF_INTERSECTION` |
| 4 | fold-back area over the ceiling | `GEOMETRY_REJECTED_DISTORTION` | `FOLD_BACK_AREA_ABOVE_CEILING` |
| 5 | coverage failures | `GEOMETRY_REJECTED_COVERAGE` | the failing checks, listed |
| 6 | a detector did not run | `GEOMETRY_UNMEASURED` | `INCOMPLETE_<stage>` |
| — | nothing fired | `GEOMETRY_CERTIFIED` | `NO_HARD_DEFECT_AT_ARTIFACT_SAMPLING` |

Four rejection states, six reasons. The reason is the useful half: two surfaces
can both be `GEOMETRY_REJECTED_DISTORTION` and have nothing in common.

> **Note** Row 6 is the fail-closed one, and it is why unmeasured exists as a
> state. No detector fired — but one did not *run*, so the surface has not been
> certified against the defects that detector would have found. Silence is not
> a pass.

> **Trap** The panel's own tooltip for `GEOMETRY_CERTIFIED` currently reads
> "passed all eight requirements of the CT/fibre gate". That sentence is not
> about this cascade — nothing above is ANDed, it is ordered, and the first
> row that fires wins. The eight requirements belong to the physical axis's own
> inner gate, covered further down this page.

### Fold-back is a fraction, not a flag

This is worth understanding before you argue with a verdict. Fold-back is
measured as the **fraction of area** that folds, and compared against
`maximum_fold_back_fraction`, which is 0.05.

It used to be an angle test, and that rejected wrinkles: the largest area this
gate would certify on a fitted winding was half a square centimetre, against
the four a campaign asks for. No angle separates a wrinkle from an error. What
the ceiling catches is a mesh folded *everywhere*, which is the failure that
matters.

### The knobs, and which move with the scroll

| Policy | Default | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `maximum_fold_back_fraction` | 0.05 | the ceiling above |
| `minimum_valid_fraction` | 0.25 | how much of the grid must carry data |
| `minimum_largest_component_fraction` | 0.90 | one sheet, not a constellation |
| `minimum_valid_triangles` | 64 | below this there is nothing to measure |
| `band_cells` | 4 | derived from `band_um` (480) and the cell step |
| `resolution_limit_voxels` | 16.0 | derived from `resolution_limit_um` (150) |

The last two are **derived from the scroll** rather than frozen: pass the voxel
size and the inter-lamina spacing and the gate recomputes them. That matters
because a grid whose step is at or above the inter-lamina spacing cannot
certify against a lamina switch at all — it cannot see one.

Every receipt says which side of that line it fell on. `resolution_limited` is
`true` when the grid's own median edge is at or above the resolution limit in
force, and `resolution_limit_measured_here` says whether that limit came from
this scroll's own measured inter-lamina spacing or from the frozen 150 µm floor
standing in for one. A `GEOMETRY_CERTIFIED` surface can carry
`resolution_limited: true` at the same time — the phase contract names that
combination as one of its three outcomes — and it means row 1 of the cascade
above could not have seen a lamina switch even if one were there.

> **Trap** Fold-back and the contact detectors are not relaxed per lane or per
> corpus. A calibration that let those through would stop protecting exactly
> where it is right.

## Reading the result

A surface is in one of three positions and the middle one is easy to miss:

- **Certified** — the gate looked and found no hard defect.
- **Rejected**, with a name — it looked and found one.
- **Unmeasured** — nobody looked.

> **Trap** Unmeasured is not a pass. Its QC job is created `WAITING_GEOMETRY`
> rather than `PENDING`, and the model can only claim `PENDING`, so the surface
> waits instead of quietly proceeding. A verdict promotes it the moment one
> arrives — waiting is not the same as stranded.

There are **two independent QC axes** and both must pass before anything
downstream may use a surface: geometry, and physical support — does the CT
actually contain material along it. `GEOMETRY_CERTIFIED` alone is not
admissible. A surface can be `CT_SUPPORTED` — the scan says papyrus is there —
and still be `GEOMETRY_REJECTED_BRIDGE`: passing physical support says nothing
about whether the mesh welded two laminae together, and the reverse combination
is just as real.

Physical support is measured by a separate, automatic worker — not by anything
this page queues — against a frozen per-scroll profile
(`surface-qc-gp-scroll1-ct-fiber-v3` is this campaign's), and it has its own
states:

| State | Means |
| --- | --- |
| `UNVALIDATED` | the default; no QC job has completed yet — waiting, not failing |
| `CT_SUPPORTED` | the CT has material along the surface; nothing was retained for ink review |
| `CT_SUPPORTED_REVIEW` | CT-supported, and the ink screen retained something for a person to look at |
| `CT_INSUFFICIENT` | no common valid pixels — the CT does not back this surface |
| `INK_SCREEN_INSUFFICIENT` | the ink screen's own result was degenerate or empty |
| `NOT_APPLICABLE_FIXTURE` | a fixture surface; not evidence, and kept out of the admissible set on purpose |

Only `CT_SUPPORTED` and `CT_SUPPORTED_REVIEW` are admissible downstream — the
same fail-closed default as geometry.

Retention for review runs candidates through a further, frozen gate of its
own: eight requirements, all **ANDed** — candidate CT coverage, central-slice
support, and the shape of the depth profile (peak count, top-3 concentration,
entropy, argmax spread). Any one failing downranks the candidate as
depth-diffuse rather than surface-localised; passing all eight only retains it
for human review, and neither outcome accepts ink, text, or a letter.

> **Note** That worker refuses before spending a GPU job when the card cannot
> be initialised at all — `GPU_UNAVAILABLE` — and puts the job back in the
> queue rather than failing it, so a card that needs a restart does not cost
> the surface its one shot.

## Lamina

A third axis, measured off the same staged copy while `certify` already has
it fetched, rather than queued separately. It asks what neither of the other
two can: does the CT resolve a single lamina under this surface, at all?
Geometry says the mesh is a plausible sheet, and says in its own non-claims
that this is not a claim about which lamina was followed; physical support
says papyrus is there somewhere along the normal. Neither reads the density
profile itself.

Three numbers decide it, against a frozen profile: bimodality (below 1.0,
there is a valley between an air mode and a papyrus mode), median thickness in
microns against a 15–70 µm band, and the fraction of columns with no missing
chunk, which has to clear 90%.

| State | Means |
| --- | --- |
| `LAMINA_SINGLE_SHEET` | thickness sits inside the band: one lamina |
| `LAMINA_FUSED` | thickness is above the band, or most columns never leave the material: more than one lamina under the sampling |
| `LAMINA_TOO_THIN` | thickness is below the band — thinner than a sheet, so the pair is unlikely to be two real interfaces |
| `LAMINA_UNRESOLVED` | no valley between an air mode and a papyrus mode anywhere on the surface: one population, or noise |
| `LAMINA_INSUFFICIENT_COLUMNS` | too few clean columns to say anything — a hole in the sampling, not a verdict |
| `LAMINA_UNMEASURED` | the default — no registered CT source, no voxel size, or the measurement failed outright |

> **Note** This axis does **not** gate P3 or P4. It is recorded beside the two
> that do, on purpose: it is a geometric measurement, not a claim about
> content. On the campaign that calibrated it, a wrap passed at 32–36 µm across
> three bands and the patch turned out to hold no ink at all. It filters where
> one *can* look, never where there is something to see.

The bands come from another campaign's own calibration, reimplemented here
from its published definitions rather than vendored — this platform's verdicts
have not been compared against that original implementation on the same input.

## Seed agreement

A separate question from certification, and a separate axis: run the same
target with two seeds and ask whether the two surfaces agree. Like lamina, it
does not gate P3 or P4 — nothing here blocks a surface from being flattened or
rendered. Four states record where a surface stands:

| State | Means |
| --- | --- |
| `SEED_UNPAIRED` | the default. Only one seed was ever run, so there is no error bar — a different thing from a small one |
| `SEED_AGREEMENT_MEASURED` | a real pair, decomposed and reported |
| `SEED_AGREEMENT_UNMEASURED` | the pair could not be compared, with the reason attached |
| `SEED_OVERRIDE_DID_NOT_TAKE` | reserved for the one failure that reads as the best possible result: two runs landing bit-identical |

The number reported is a Chamfer distance, **decomposed** into a component along
the surface and one along its normal, and the headline is the **normal** one —
it answers at what depth the ink is being sampled. It is normalised by lamina
thickness — this surface's own measured thickness from the lamina axis above
when there is one, or a 35.5 µm reference otherwise — not winding pitch.

> **Note** Three numbers are reported and the total is never shown alone. A
> total of 121 µm over a 371 µm winding pitch is 0.33; a normal of 12 µm over a
> 35.5 µm sheet thickness is 0.338. They round alike and mean different things,
> so the unit says which one it is.

> **Trap** A Chamfer of zero is refused, not reported as a pass. Two runs
> landing on the same bytes mean the seed override never reached the
> optimizer — a different failure from `SEED_UNPAIRED`: this is two runs that
> happened and agreed too well to be real, not one run that never got a
> partner.

Seed agreement measures **reproducibility, not correctness**. Two fits can agree
perfectly on the wrong sheet.

## Traps

> **Trap** Reading certification as "this is correct". It means no hard defect
> was found at the sampling the gate uses.

> **Trap** Assuming an imported surface was checked because it is in the
> catalogue. It arrived with a bounding box and a hash and no verdict.
